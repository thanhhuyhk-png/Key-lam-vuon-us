Your aloe can survive weeks without water, but three to five feet from a west-facing window, it turns bronze and crispy. That's not a watering mistake—that's heat damage. **What you'll discover:** Aloe vera closes its leaf pores to lock in water during drought. But those same pores are its air conditioning system. When direct sun heats them behind glass, the leaf temperature climbs with nowhere to cool down. That's when the green pigment breaks down, turning the leaf bronze or brown. The fix isn't complicated—move it back three to five feet, give it bright indirect light, and watch new growth come in clean and plump. This works for coastal zones and scorching desert climates alike.
