### THE 7 SWAPS (2:00–14:00)
Mỗi swap: ❌ Old advice → ✅ The swap → Why it still keeps aloe healthy → quick proof/number.

**Swap 1 — Lightweight pot instead of heavy terracotta (2:00–4:00)**
- ❌ "Only terracotta breathes." ✅ Resin/plastic pot + drainage holes.
- Why safe: "Lighter on your back, and with the right soil the breathing problem disappears — the soil does the draining, not the pot." [SOURCE: succulentsandsunshine, bobvila — verify 1.5]
- Number: "A 10-inch terracotta pot full of soil can hit 15+ pounds. The same pot in resin? Under 5." [NEED DATA: confirm weights]

**Swap 2 — High-perlite soil mix (compensates for plastic) (4:00–6:00)** 🔴 mid-roll dip zone → tension layer here
- ✅ Extra perlite/pumice (about half the mix) so plastic drains as fast as clay.
- Pattern break: simple "shoes that fit" analogy (brand voice). "Heavy soil is like shoes two sizes too small — the roots can't breathe."
- Accessibility Anchor: "One bag of perlite lasts you years."

**Swap 3 — No-lift moisture check (4 → the surprise) (6:00–8:00)**
- ❌ "Lift the pot to feel if it's light." ✅ Wooden skewer / chopstick test OR a $8 moisture meter.
- "Push a skewer to the bottom, pull it out. Clean and dry? Water. Damp with soil stuck on? Wait. No lifting, no guessing." (Accessibility Anchor)

**Swap 4 — Waist-height plant stand instead of high shelf (8:00–9:30)**
- ❌ "Put it on a high shelf away from pets." ✅ Waist-height stand — no climbing, no step stool (fall risk), and still above curious pets.
- Pet safety beat (Theme 3 — quick win): "Aloe is mildly toxic to cats and dogs if they chew it [SOURCE: ASPCA], so waist height keeps it out of reach of small dogs without you ever needing a stool."

**Swap 5 — Rolling caddy for the seasonal move (9:30–11:00)**
- ❌ "Carry it indoors before frost." ✅ Plant caddy with wheels — roll, don't lift.
- USDA zone beat (Theme 2): "In zones 9–11 aloe lives outside year-round. Colder than that, it comes in before first frost — and a $10 caddy means you just roll it to the door."

**Swap 6 — One-handed harvest with kitchen shears (11:00–12:30)**
- ❌ Twisting/snapping thick leaves (needs grip strength). ✅ Long-handled kitchen shears, cut at base, one hand.
- "No grip strength needed, no bending if your plant's at waist height."

**Swap 7 (BONUS) — Sharing pups with the grandkids (12:30–14:00)**
- Theme 1 (nostalgia GOLD): "One aloe makes babies — little pups at the base. Twist one off, pot it in a paper cup, and you've got a gift for a grandchild's first windowsill."
- Ties to legacy/multigenerational. Light, warm.
