Most people miss the real culprit when roses don't bloom. It's not fertilizer or water—it's what happens before the rose ever touches the ground. When nursery roses stay in their pots too long, the roots start circling instead of growing outward. Even if you straighten them out, those roots still want to coil back up, choking the plant instead of feeding it. Extension offices call it being root-bound.

This video pulls straight from university extension research to show you exactly how to fix it. Learn the planting mistake that quietly holds your roses back for years, the soil amendment that gives roots room to breathe, and why that spoonful of finely ground eggshells outperforms whole shells by a full season. One viewer in Oregon tried this combination on a rose bush that hadn't bloomed well in two years—she reported it looked better in June than it had in years.

Remember: roses need 6-8 hours of direct sun a day to bloom well, and they'll reward you for it.
