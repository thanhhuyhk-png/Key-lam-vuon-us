## YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION

How to keep snakes out of your yard — without tearing out your garden. In this video, you'll learn how to read your yard like a snake map using a simple 3-zone system that tells you exactly where to start and what to fix first.

In this video, you'll learn:
- Why snakes keep coming back (it's not the plants — it's the microclimate they create)
- The 3-zone audit system: Foundation Perimeter, Mid-Garden, and Border/Fence Line
- The "daylight test" that takes 5 minutes and reduces shelter in Zone One immediately
- How to build a living fence using rosemary, lemongrass, and marigolds — and why each one works differently
- What to do this weekend to make your yard less inviting to snakes — no special tools required

Most homeowners Google "which plants attract snakes" and get a list with no system. This video gives you the system. Snakes are attracted to three things: cover from predators, moisture regulation, and prey. Any plant that creates those conditions is a problem — regardless of its name. When you understand that, you stop chasing a list and start redesigning three edges of your yard.

Zone One is your foundation perimeter — the highest-risk zone and the fastest to fix. Zone Two is your mid-garden, where structural complexity like ornamental grasses and dense shrubs create year-round shelter. Zone Three is your perimeter fence line — the one zone where you can act offensively with a living aromatic barrier.

If you have kids or pets, Zone One is your first twenty minutes this weekend.
