From bare patches under walnut trees to mysterious seedling failure, allelopathy solves a lot of garden struggles. This video ranks 10 plants that secretly poison their neighbors, sorted by actual danger level. Black walnut fires juglone through roots up to 80 feet away; garlic mustard sneaks through underground fungi disruption; Japanese knotweed floods the soil with multiple root compounds. But not all garden crimes are real—Norway maple gets wrongly blamed for chemistry it isn't doing. Plus: the truth about mint, ivy, and the pine needle acidification myth that university research already debunked.

The fix isn't "rip it out." Plant things that tolerate the chemistry—daylilies and hostas under walnut work fine. Use barriers for sensitive crops. And always dig first to check the roots before you blame the plant.
