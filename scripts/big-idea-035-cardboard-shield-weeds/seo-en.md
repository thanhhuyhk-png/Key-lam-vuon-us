Kill Weeds With Cardboard — No Bending, No Pulling, $0 Cost

Stop bending over every summer to pull the same weeds back. Texas A&M AgriLife researchers confirmed that plain cardboard blocks the light weeds need to grow — and it costs $0. Learn exactly how to lay it down without bending, overlap the seams properly, and keep it in place all season.

**In This Video, You'll Learn:**
- Which cardboard type works best (and which to avoid)
- How to deploy sheets from standing position without bending
- The 6–8 inch overlap rule that stops weeds from sneaking through
- Why light deprivation kills weed growth at the source
- What to layer on top to hide cardboard and secure everything

Most weed seeds need light to sprout—that's the science behind sheet mulching. Cardboard creates a blackout layer blocking the light weeds and new seeds need to grow. Research from Texas A&M AgriLife, UC Cooperative Extension, and University of Connecticut Extension confirms this works best with whole cardboard sheets overlapped 6–8 inches at seams. Use a long-handled rake to position each sheet while standing. Top with three inches of mulch (wood chips, straw, or shredded leaves), water it in, and the cardboard breaks down naturally over a season, enriching soil with organic matter. By next spring, the ground will be soft enough to plant through. Choose plain, unbleached cardboard and remove all tape, labels, and staples beforehand.

This method excels against annual weeds sprouting fresh each spring. Perennial weeds with deep roots—bindweed, Bermuda grass, quackgrass—are tougher and may need 3–5 years of consistent cardboard coverage to break down their root systems. USDA-funded research from University of Minnesota measured significant reductions in weed biomass in real test plots.

One weekend of setup. One season of peace for your back.
