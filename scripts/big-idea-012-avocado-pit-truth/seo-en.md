**Growing avocado from seed** — the honest truth about what really happens when you plant that pit from your kitchen.

Most people don't know that the seed inside won't grow the same avocado you just ate. It's a genetic shuffle, and out of every five hundred seedlings, barely one is worth keeping. But here's the better news: you can grow a stunning, glossy houseplant for free — in just a pot and some potting soil. Forget the toothpicks.

In this video, you'll learn:
- Why that avocado pit will almost never give you fruit (and why that's okay)
- How to skip the toothpicks and plant directly in soil — the lazy gardener's way
- The one pinch that turns a bare stick into a full, lush plant
- How long you'll really wait (spoiler: it's glacial)
- The one honest path to homegrown avocado fruit: grafting

This five-part breakdown walks you through what university extension offices actually say about growing avocados indoors. You'll learn the real timeline, why your last attempt probably failed, and how to end up with something beautiful either way — whether it's a gorgeous green houseplant or a future fruit-bearer.

Perfect for anyone who's tossed an avocado pit and wondered, "Could I actually grow that?" The answer is yes. You just need to know which prize you're really chasing.

**Important Pet Safety Note:** The avocado plant is toxic to pets. All parts — leaves, bark, pit, fruit — contain persin. Leaves are especially toxic to birds and can be deadly in small amounts. Keep your plant well out of reach of cats, dogs, birds, and other pets. If a pet eats any part of the plant, call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center immediately.
