### BACK LAYER — 3 Structural Anchors (2:30–6:00)

*Role: Height, permanence, winter interest. These never leave. They're the bones.*

**Plant 1: Russian Sage** *(Perovskia atriplicifolia)*
- Zones 3–9 | 3–5 ft tall | Blooms: June through October
- Silvery stems, lavender-blue spires — looks architectural even before it blooms
- Deer resistant, drought tolerant once established
- Tip: Don't cut it back in fall — silver stems carry winter interest and protect the crown.

**Plant 2: Karl Foerster Grass** *(Calamagrostis x acutiflora)*
- Zones 5–9 | 4–5 ft | Feathery plumes: June through winter
- Vertical exclamation point — moves in the wind, catches light all season
- Tip: One plant becomes a 3-ft wide clump by year 3. Give it 30 inches of space upfront.

**Plant 3: Joe Pye Weed** *(Eutrochium purpureum)*
- Zones 4–9 | 4–6 ft | Blooms: July–September (mauve-pink domes)
- Native plant — pollinators mob it in August
- Tip: Looks weedy until July — it's doing root work underground. Don't pull it.

---

### MIDDLE LAYER — 5 Succession Bloomers (6:00–12:00)

*Role: The baton pass. Each plant hands color to the next, May through October.*

**Plant 4: Catmint 'Walker's Low'** *(Nepeta racemosa)*
- Zones 4–8 | 18–24 inches | Blooms: May–June, reblooms August–September
- Lavender-blue clouds that billow over the middle zone
- Tip: Shear by 1/3 in July after first bloom → guaranteed second flush in 6 weeks.

**Plant 5: Salvia 'May Night'** *(Salvia nemorosa)*
- Zones 4–8 | 18 inches | Blooms: May–July (deep violet spikes)
- Deadhead spent spikes → often reflowers August–September
- Tip: Pair directly with Catmint — same bloom window, contrasting tones create depth.

**Plant 6: Purple Coneflower** *(Echinacea purpurea)*
- Zones 3–9 | 2–3 ft | Blooms: June–October
- Blooms 4 months straight — the workhorse of the middle layer
- Tip: Don't deadhead. Faded flowers still look intentional and feed birds through December.

**Plant 7: Black-Eyed Susan** *(Rudbeckia fulgida 'Goldsturm')*
- Zones 3–9 | 18–24 inches | Blooms: July–October
- Bright gold daisy-like flowers — summer's defining color moment
- Tip: Plant where the Catmint gap will be in July — they alternate perfectly in the timeline.

**Plant 8: Sedum 'Autumn Joy'** *(Hylotelephium 'Herbstfreude')*
- Zones 3–9 | 18–24 inches | Blooms: August–October (dusty pink → copper)
- Starts as a structural succulent mound, ends as a fall centerpiece
- Tip: Skip watering during drought — it gets tougher and more dramatic.

---

### FRONT LAYER — 3 Ground-Level Fillers (12:00–15:00)

*Role: Low-growing, dense coverage. Crowd out weeds. Open and close the season.*

**Plant 9: Creeping Phlox** *(Phlox subulata)*
- Zones 3–9 | 4–6 inches | Blooms: April–May (first color of the season)
- Carpet of magenta, lavender, or white — opens the show before everything else wakes up
- Spreads 2 ft wide per plant over 3 years — self-mulching ground cover
- Tip: Plant 3 at front edge, 18 inches apart. By year 2 they close the front line entirely.

**Plant 10: Coreopsis 'Moonbeam'** *(Coreopsis verticillata)*
- Zones 4–9 | 12–15 inches | Blooms: June–September
- Soft yellow, thread-like foliage — the filler that never looks fussy
- Blooms continuously without deadheading
- Tip: Fills the height gap between Creeping Phlox (4 in) and middle layer (18+ in).

**Plant 11: New England Aster** *(Symphyotrichum novae-angliae)*
- Zones 3–8 | 18–24 inches | Blooms: September–October
- Season closer — purple/pink/white daisy-like flowers when everything else winds down
- Monarch butterflies use it as a migration fuel stop in October
- Tip: Pinch back tips once in late June to keep it compact and bushy, not leggy.

---

### THE SPACING RULE (15:00–15:45)

**"Touch but don't overlap."**

- Front layer: 18 inches on-center
- Middle layer: 20–24 inches on-center
- Back layer: 24–30 inches on-center
- Total plant count: ~28 plants across 11 varieties
- Year 1: mulch gaps 2–3 inches deep. Year 3: canopy closes, weeds suppressed naturally.
