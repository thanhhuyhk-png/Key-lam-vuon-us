## YouTube Description

**Low light indoor plants for dark rooms** — discover 7 houseplants that actually survive in the darkest corner of your house without windows. No dead zones in your home anymore.

Every house has that one corner — a windowless bathroom, a dark hallway, or a spot behind heavy curtains — where you think no plant can live. You're wrong. This video shows you seven plants that do well in light levels so low, most houseplants would struggle. Some survive at just 25 foot-candles of light.

**In this video, you'll learn:**
- How to identify if a room is truly dark enough to need special plants
- 7 houseplants for dark rooms and what light levels each one actually needs (25–75 foot-candles)
- Which indoor plants are safe around dogs and cats — and which ones to keep up high
- Why the ZZ plant survives at light levels other houseplants can't even register
- Practical ways to care for low-light plants without a grow light

**Here's what these seven plants have in common.** Cast iron plant, Chinese evergreen, parlor palm, snake plant, pothos, heartleaf philodendron, and ZZ plant all handle shade that kills most houseplants. The cast iron plant earned its name from thriving on factory floors during the Industrial Revolution — bartenders even watered it with leftover beer. The Chinese evergreen adds color with silver-speckled leaves. The parlor palm is completely non-toxic, making it perfect if you have curious pets. But the snake plant and pothos? Both are toxic to dogs and cats, so they belong on a shelf up high.

The real standout is the ZZ plant. University of Florida researchers tracked it surviving — and still producing new leaves — for over a year at just 25 foot-candles of light. That's half the minimum most extension offices consider survivable for houseplants. Even so, it's not magic: leaving any plant in total darkness around the clock will eventually kill it. The trick is rotating plants every four to eight weeks to a brighter spot, or using even a regular room light on a timer to push those light levels into the survivable range.

This video breaks down exactly which room is darkest, which plant belongs where, and how to make it work without the guesswork.
