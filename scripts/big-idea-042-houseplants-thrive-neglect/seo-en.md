```
Overwatering houseplants is the real killer, not forgetting to water them. A survey of houseplant owners found that watering too much, not too little, is the most common way people accidentally kill their own plants. Here are five houseplants that actually do better on a lighter watering schedule, and the science behind why "caring more" often backfires.

In this video you'll learn:
- Five common houseplants that store their own water and thrive on neglect: ZZ plant, jade plant, ponytail palm, cast iron plant, and dracaena
- The exact watering schedule for each one, from every two weeks to every ten
- Why an overwatered plant and an underwatered plant show the exact same symptoms, according to university extension research
- A ten-second finger test that tells you whether to water, no calendar required
- Which three of these five are toxic to pets, and which two are completely safe

Here's the twist this video is built around: extension researchers say a plant that's drowning and a plant that's thirsty look almost identical from the outside. Yellow leaves, droopy stems, the same story either way. So when a well-meaning plant owner sees droopy leaves and waters again, they can actually be finishing off a plant that was already overwatered. Each of the five plants covered here carries its own water reservoir, a swollen stem, thick leaves, or a root built to store water, which means watering on a fixed schedule often does more harm than good.

Every plant here is common in American homes. Pet owners: three of these five, the ZZ plant, the jade plant, and the dracaena, are on the ASPCA's toxic list, covered in detail near the end, along with the two that are completely safe for cats and dogs.
```
