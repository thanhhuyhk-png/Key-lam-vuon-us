## YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION

Grow fresh herbs in water with zero soil, zero smell, and zero mess — just a jar, a windowsill, and a twice-weekly habit backed by CDC mosquito science. In this video, I walk you through seven herbs that thrive in water alone: mint, basil, thyme, chives, oregano, parsley, and lemon balm.

**What you'll learn in this video:**
- The 7 easiest herbs to root and grow in water (and why rosemary isn't one of them)
- The exact twice-a-week water-change schedule that stops smell, algae, and mosquitoes before they start
- How the CDC mosquito life cycle proves your jar stays pest-free
- Which herbs root fastest (mint and basil — you'll see roots in 7-10 days)
- Why patience with lemon balm pays off (and when most people give up)
- The simple vinegar trick to clean algae off glass jars without chemicals
- A critical safety note if you have cats or dogs in the house

**The Science:**
A mosquito takes 7–10 days to go from egg to biting adult. By changing your jar water twice a week (Sunday and Wednesday, for example), you dump out the entire water before any mosquito can complete its life cycle — which means no smell, no bugs, no surprise breeding ground on your kitchen counter. This routine also keeps roots healthy and prevents the cloudy, slimy jar problem that scares people away from water propagation.

**Why this matters for seniors:**
No digging, no bags of soil to lift, no kneeling on a bad knee. Just snip a cutting from your kitchen, drop it in water, and you're growing fresh herbs for months. A bunch of basil or mint at the grocery store wilts in a week and costs three or four dollars. These seven, once rooted, keep producing for free.

**Easy next step:**
Grab whatever herb is sitting in your kitchen right now — a mint stem from a bouquet, a basil cutting — and drop it into a glass of water today. In a week, you'll see white roots. In a month, you'll be snipping fresh leaves.
