Over twenty years ago, a gardener in New York boiled down an entire seed-starting method into five words: starting plants outdoors, in winter. It worked so well the method's listed right in the national agricultural glossary — no grow lights, no heat mats, nothing fancy. Step one's easy: wash out a gallon milk jug and toss the cap so rain and snow can slip right inside. Cold, damp winter weather breaks dormancy naturally, and your seedlings wake up tough come spring. Fair warning, though — it isn't for every seed packet. Six cold-hardy crops love winter jugs, three favorites don't, and three sit right on the fence. Let's prep your jug first. Grab a clean jug you washed in warm, soapy water and start at the bottom. You'll need real drainage down there, or melting snow'll turn the whole thing into a swamp and drown your seeds.
