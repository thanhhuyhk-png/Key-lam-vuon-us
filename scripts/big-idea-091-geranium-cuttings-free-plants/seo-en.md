## SEO DESCRIPTION (EN)

**Take geranium cuttings now** — before frost turns your porch pots into compost. A three-to-four-inch piece of stem becomes a whole new plant by spring, and it costs you nothing but a knife and patience.

In this video, you'll learn:
- Where to cut: three to four inches from the tip
- How to pick a healthy plant and stem, and which plants to skip
- The one step two sources recommend and four never mention: letting the cut heal
- Which clean mixes work (and why to skip dirt from the yard)
- How long to wait for roots: sources give three windows, all including six weeks
- Three ways to bring a geranium through winter, and the only one that gives you more plants

Taking geraniums indoors for winter keeps them from freezing, but hauling whole pots takes space. This video walks you through the lazy gardener's way: take cuttings now, get free plants next spring, and keep your porch from looking bare. The basics are simple. The one step the sources split on is whether to let the cut end heal before planting, and you'll see exactly what they say and how to test it on your own windowsill.
