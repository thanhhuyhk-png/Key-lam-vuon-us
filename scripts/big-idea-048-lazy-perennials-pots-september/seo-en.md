6 Lazy Perennials for Pots — Plant Once, Done Forever

Here's the good news: these six hardy perennials grow in containers year-round, coming back stronger each season with zero replanting needed. No kneeling, no bending, no bare pots staring back at you in April.

In this video, you'll learn:
- 6 low-maintenance container perennials (hardy zone 3–10, sourced from university extension guides)
- How to winterize potted plants (3 proven methods from extension offices)
- The exact soil mix and pot sizes (including why drainage matters for sedum and lavender)
- Plant toxicity info (which ones are safe for pets, and which need caution)

The six plants—coreopsis, heuchera (coral bells), hosta, lavender, dwarf ornamental grass, and sedum—each solve a different gardening problem. Coreopsis blooms all summer in cheerful yellow with nearly zero care. Heuchera gives year-round foliage color in shade (purple, lime, even near-black). Hosta is the beloved classic that's lived in American shade gardens for decades. Lavender and dwarf ornamental grass thrive in warmer zones (5+), while sedum is the toughest of all—barely asking for anything except well-drained soil.

Here's what nobody mentions: a pot doesn't insulate roots like real ground does. That's why extension offices recommend choosing plants hardy two zones colder than your location. We cover three winterization methods—sinking pots into the ground, moving them to an unheated garage (20–45°F), or burying them in straw—plus the extension-recommended soil mix (1 part garden soil, 1 part peat moss, 1 part perlite, heavier on perlite for lavender and sedum).

If you've already seen our no-dig perennials for the ground, these six are the pot version of that same idea: beautiful gardens, less labor.
