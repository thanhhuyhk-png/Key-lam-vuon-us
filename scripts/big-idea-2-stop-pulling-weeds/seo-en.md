## YouTube Description

Stop pulling these 4 weeds — they're running a chemical defense system your garden actually needs. Science finally explains what your grandmother already knew.

In this video, you'll learn:
- Why mugwort and wormwood were protected by traditional farmers for centuries — and the US patent that proves they were right
- How French marigolds suppress root-knot nematodes underground using one of the most potent natural compounds ever documented
- The truth about lemongrass as a garden border plant — what the research actually shows (and what it doesn't)
- Exactly how to grow each of these plants without letting them take over your garden

Every spring, millions of gardeners spend money killing mugwort and wormwood — the exact plants that Appalachian and Eastern European farmers carefully kept at their garden edges for generations. Not out of habit. Out of hard-won strategy.

This video walks through the peer-reviewed chemistry behind four plants most gardeners treat as weeds: mugwort (camphor and cineole disrupt snake chemoreception, per US Patent 6,689,397), wormwood (absinthin secreted through roots suppresses competing plants in a two-foot radius), lemongrass (citral — the active compound in commercial insect repellents — documented by Cornell's NYSIPM program), and French marigold (alpha-terthienyl penetrates nematode hypodermis, Biology Open, 2019).

Your grandmother didn't have the vocabulary. Now you do. Work smarter, not harder.
