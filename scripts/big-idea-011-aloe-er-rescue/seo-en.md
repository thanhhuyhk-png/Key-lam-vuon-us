## DESCRIPTION

Save a dying aloe vera — even one with black, mushy, rotting roots. Most people throw a rotting aloe in the trash, but here's the rule that changes everything: if there's still green, there's still something to save. This is the complete 5-step root rot rescue, made simple for beginners and senior gardeners.

In this video, you'll learn:
- How to diagnose a dying aloe in 60 seconds — root rot vs. underwatering vs. sunburn
- The "amputation" step almost everyone gets backwards (and why it saves the plant)
- How to callus, repot, and nurse a rootless aloe back to life
- Why you must NOT water it right away — and the science behind aloe's toughness
- What recovery really looks like, week by week, season by season

A dying aloe is rarely as dead as it looks. Overwatering is the most common cause, and the soft, smelly base it leaves behind scares most people into giving up too soon. But aloe vera is one of the toughest houseplants on Earth — it stores its own water inside those fat leaves and can regrow roots from almost nothing. Follow these five steps — diagnose, decide, cut, callus, and wait — and you'll give your plant a real second chance. It's the same patient, low-fuss approach that kept our grandmothers' aloes alive for thirty years.
