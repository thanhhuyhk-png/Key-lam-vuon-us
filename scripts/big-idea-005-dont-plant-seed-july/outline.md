## Research Notes

- Paper towel/wet towel germination test procedure (10 seeds, damp towel, sealed bag/container, warm spot >70°F) — UAF Cooperative Extension: https://www.uaf.edu/ces/publications/database/gardening/procedures-wet-towel-germ.php ; Illinois Extension: https://extension.illinois.edu/blogs/good-growing/2023-01-20-are-my-seeds-still-good-testing-seed-germination
- Many seeds germinate within 7-10 days; fast sprouters (cucumber) 3-5 days; slow (peppers) up to 3 weeks; extend test 5 more days if seeds still swollen/just starting — general extension sources (see WebSearch results, need exact .edu citation at research-brief step)
- Germination vs viability distinction — viability can decline before germination fails entirely (old seeds may sprout weak seedlings) — Illinois Extension: https://extension.illinois.edu/blogs/ilriverhort/2017-02-03-test-seed-viability
- Seed storage longevity: basil/broccoli/cabbage/cauliflower/tomatoes 4-5 years; beans/carrots 3 years; aster/sage 1-2 years — Iowa State Extension: https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/how-store-seeds-and-test-germination-rates
- Interpreting results: 100% germinate → plant normally; 70-90% → still usable, sow heavier; <50-60% → buy new seed — Iowa State Extension (same link); corroborated by Clemson HGIC: https://hgic.clemson.edu/are-those-old-vegetable-seed-still-good/
- Germination % benchmarks by crop type: beans/corn/squash/tomatoes/broccoli/cabbage good above 75%; some flowers 45-60% considered good — need exact .edu source confirmation at research-brief step
- July midsummer planting for fall harvest: ~90-day window before first frost if crop matures under 90 days and isn't cold-hardy — UMN Extension: https://extension.umn.edu/planting-and-growing-guides/planting-vegetables-midsummer-fall-harvest
- Poor germination causes (age/viability, planting depth, soil temp <50°F for warm-season crops) — University of Maryland Extension: https://extension.umd.edu/resource/poor-germination-vegetable-seeds
