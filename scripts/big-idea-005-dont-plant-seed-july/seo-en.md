**Paper Towel Seed Test — Don't Waste Your Fall Garden on Dead Seeds**

Before you plant this July, you need to know if your old seeds are still alive. A single damp paper towel can tell you the truth in days, and it costs nothing. In this video, you'll learn the exact same germination test that extension offices recommend, how to read the results, and why timing matters when you're racing the frost.

**In this video, you'll learn:**
- How to set up a simple paper towel test in 5 minutes (everything's already in your kitchen)
- Why seeds look fine on the outside but die silently inside from age
- Exactly how long different seeds stay good in storage (tomatoes 4 years, peppers just 1–2)
- Why testing on day three might throw out perfectly good beans
- How to read your test results and know exactly how many extra seeds to sow
- The real reason judging by looks will cost you your fall harvest
- The humidity and temperature rule that keeps seeds viable for years

**Why this matters for July planting:** You've got about 90 days before the first frost. Every seed that doesn't come up is garden space sitting empty while that window closes. Testing first turns a guess into a real number you can plan around—that's the difference between a full bed and a thin one in late summer.

The paper towel test isn't a grandma trick. It's what university extension programs teach. No soil, no trays, no guessing. Just ten seeds, one paper towel, and a zip bag. This video walks you through setup, timing by crop (fast movers like radish in 3 days vs. slow ones like peppers in 3 weeks), and how to handle the results so you plant with confidence instead of hope.

Bonus at the end: the simple math that doubles how long your seeds stay good—and why a sealed jar in the fridge beats a plastic bag on the shelf every single time.
