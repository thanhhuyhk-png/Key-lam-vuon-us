**Seated Gardening Setup — Exact Measurements**

The height of your garden bed is everything. Here's the rule:
- **Regular chair (side approach):** Soil surface 24–28 inches high
- **Wheelchair (forward approach):** Soil surface 28–34 inches high, with knee clearance at least 27 inches tall and 30 inches wide underneath
- **University of Maryland Salad Table:** Approximately 36 inches high, with adjustable legs to fit you
- **Bed width:** Maximum 24 inches if reaching from one side; up to 48 inches if reaching from both sides
- **Aisle/pathway:** At least 48 inches wide for safe wheelchair or walker access

Get the height right, and all seven of these plants come right to you — no bending, no kneeling, no aches afterward.

**Each Plant at a Glance:**
1. **Leaf Lettuce** — 40 days to harvest, 4–6 inches of soil, cut-and-come-again (regrows in 2–3 weeks)
2. **Radishes** — 25–35 days, same shallow soil, quick wins
3. **Swiss Chard** — 50–70 days, cut the outer leaves and it keeps producing until frost
4. **Dwarf Cherry Tomato** — 45–65 days, 5-gallon pot, stays 2–4.5 feet tall (no tall trellis needed)
5. **Bush Beans** — 55–65 days, self-supporting (no climbing poles), 3–4 plants per pot
6. **Strawberries** — Hanging basket or tall pot, fruit dangles down for easy picking, day-neutral varieties fruit all season
7. **Herbs** — Basil, parsley, chives, mint: snip while you sit, regrow with each cut

**Watering Made Easy**

Raised beds and pots dry out fast when they're off the ground. Two solutions:
- **Self-watering containers:** Water is wicked up from a reservoir at the base through the soil by capillary action — you just refill every few days instead of watering daily
- **Soaker hose + timer:** Set once, waters automatically — no hauling a heavy watering can around

**Pet Safety — Important if You Have Cats or Dogs**

Chives, mint, parsley, and tomato leaves are toxic to pets. Basil and strawberries are safe.
- **Keep toxic plants out of reach** if you have a cat or dog
- **If your pet nibbles on chives, mint, parsley, or tomato leaves, call your vet or ASPCA Poison Control immediately: (888) 426-4435**
- Ripe tomatoes are perfectly safe for pets; only the leaves and stems are toxic

**Staying Safe While Gardening**

Falls are a real risk — the CDC reports that one in four adults over 65 falls each year. When you stand up after sitting and tending your garden:
- Rise slowly to avoid dizziness
- Use a chair with armrests so you have something solid to push up from
- Wear shoes with good grip, not loose slippers
- Keep your phone within reach
