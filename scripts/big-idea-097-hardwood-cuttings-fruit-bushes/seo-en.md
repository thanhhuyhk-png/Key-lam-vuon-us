## YouTube SEO Description (EN)

Fruit bushes and vines can start from winter cuttings: grapes, currants, gooseberries, elderberries, figs and pomegranates. Take a piece of last year's dormant wood, keep it cool, and start it when the plant and the weather are ready. It's free if you already own the parent plant, or a neighbor says yes.

Not every fruit tree works this way. Apples rarely root from cuttings and are usually grafted. And some named varieties are plant-patented, so check the tag before you cut.

PET AND FOOD SAFETY: The ASPCA says grapes and raisins can cause kidney damage in dogs, so keep the fruit away from them. Fig sap can irritate skin in sunlight, and fig is listed as toxic to cats, dogs and horses. Cook ripe elderberries before eating, and skip unripe berries, leaves and stems. Not sure? Ask your local extension office or your vet.

CHECK YOUR STATE: Some states restrict currants and gooseberries because of white pine blister rust. Ask your state agriculture office before planting.
