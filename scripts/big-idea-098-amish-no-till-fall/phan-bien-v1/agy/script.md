### H1 · Habit one: snip, don't pull

Habit one: snip, don't pull.

Walk out to that tired tomato bed. The vines are brown and tired, and the bed looks ready for a rest. Your first thought is to yank everything out by the roots and leave the bed clean.

Instead, cut each plant at the soil line. Leave the roots right where they are. [C31] All you need is a pair of pruners. No digging.

Here's why this works. Those roots rot in place over winter and add organic matter. [C31] And as they break down, they leave little channels in the soil. Earthworms use those channels, and so does water. [C34] It's free plumbing, and you didn't lift a shovel.

Now, one exception, and it matters. If a plant had a disease, like spotted, wilting leaves, pull it and bag it. Put it in the trash, not the compost pile. [C32] Some soil diseases hang on for years, so move that crop to a new spot next season. [C33]

So here's your first step. This weekend, snip your spent plants at the soil line instead of pulling them. It takes a few minutes per bed.

### H4 · Habit four: plant right through it

Habit four: plant right through it.

In spring, push the mulch aside where you want to plant. Make a furrow for seeds, or dig a small hole for a transplant. [C23] Once the plants are up, pull the mulch back around them. [C23]

Now the weed test. Here's why this works. Weed seeds only sprout from about the top two inches of soil.
