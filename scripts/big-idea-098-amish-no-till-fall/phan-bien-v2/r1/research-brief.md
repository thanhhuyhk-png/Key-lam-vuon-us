## Consensus Principles (3)

**1. Tilling destroys the biological glue that holds soil together**
Tilling breaks the aggregates that fungi produce, eliminating the pore space that roots, water, and air depend on. Healthy no-till soil has the sponge-like structure; tilled soil reverts to powder.
- Hội tụ: UNL Lancaster Extension (T1) + OSU Extension (T1) + UNH Extension (T1)
- Khác nhau: Penn State compaction article (T1) focuses on mechanical compaction (heavy equipment), not tillage; mechanism is the same but scale differs.
- Mã claim: C01, C02, C04, C05, C44
- URLs: lancaster.unl.edu · extension.oregonstate.edu/news/no-till-gardening-builds-healthier-soil-home · extension.unh.edu/node/5769

**2. Tilling brings buried weed seeds to the surface; undisturbed soil plus mulch shrinks the weed seed bank over time**
Every pass of a tiller or plow reaches into the seed bank and delivers dormant seeds to the germination zone. No-till keeps them buried in darkness. Thick mulch additionally blocks light from any seeds near the surface.
- Hội tụ: UNL Lancaster Extension (T1) + AHDB (T3) + MSU E-2717 (T1, STALE 2000)
- Khác nhau: AHDB measures this in arable ploughing (not home garden tilling), so the 35%-brought-up figure applies directionally but not exactly to a 6-inch tiller. No-till creates a surface seed concentration (C28) which could mean first-year no-till actually sees more surface-weed emergence before the bank depletes.
- Mã claim: C03, C22, C27, C28, C24
- URLs: lancaster.unl.edu · ahdb.org.uk/knowledge-library/how-cultivating-affects-the-arable-weed-seedbank · archive.lib.msu.edu/DMC/extension_publications/e2717/E2717-2000.PDF

**3. Fall is the right time to add organic matter and raw manure to vegetable beds; apply early enough for 120 days before spring harvest**
Three independent extension programs all name fall as the preferred season. The 120-day pre-harvest rule (USDA NOP, confirmed by Penn State) makes fall application the safest window for raw manure before a spring root-contact crop.
- Hội tụ: Penn State Extension (T1) + UNL Lancaster Extension (T1) + SDSU Extension (T1)
- Khác nhau: SDSU says "manure must be at least six months old" — this refers to the manure's state, not the timing before harvest. Penn State's 120 days is the harvest-interval rule. Both can apply simultaneously (old manure, applied at the right time).
- Mã claim: C08, C09, C10, C16, C17
- URLs: extension.psu.edu/wise-use-of-manure-in-home-vegetable-gardens · lancaster.unl.edu · extension.sdstate.edu/fall-garden-cleanup

A farmer in Leacock Township, Lancaster County, PA told a WITF reporter he still farms with horses. His neighbor's farmland went from $8,000 per acre to $20,000 per acre over 30 years — and now only about one-third of Lancaster County's 39,000+ Amish still farm.
