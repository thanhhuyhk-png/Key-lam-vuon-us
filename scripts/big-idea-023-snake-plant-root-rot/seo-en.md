## YouTube SEO DESCRIPTION

Snake plant root rot doesn't happen because you forgot to water it — it happens because of five habits that look like good care. Even though snake plants are famous for surviving almost anything, root rot is still the #1 reason they die. This video breaks down each mistake, shows you why it happens underground, and walks you through a rescue plan if your plant is already mushy at the base.

**In this video, you'll learn:**
- Why pot size matters more than you think — and the simple rule to follow
- How water in the wrong spot (the rosette or crown) starts root rot even when you're trying to be careful
- The exact soil mix that drains fast enough to prevent fungal infections like Pythium and Phytophthora
- Why low light changes everything about your watering schedule — same routine, completely different outcome
- How to tell the difference between "I'm watering on a schedule" and "I'm watering when my plant needs it"
- Step-by-step rescue protocol if your snake plant's roots are already black or mushy
- Why cinnamon powder has real antifungal power (documented in lab studies) — but it's NOT a rooting hormone
- Pet safety: why snake plants are toxic to dogs and cats, and what to do if yours investigates

The five mistakes come down to one thing: standing water in the soil. Pythium zoospores swim through wet soil to attack roots, Phytophthora thrives in damp, oxygen-starved conditions, and southern blight targets the base of the plant where the stem meets soil. Your grandmother's "unkillable" snake plant earned that reputation because it forgives neglect — but it can't forgive the one environment it wasn't built for: wet feet.

This video isn't about never watering again. It's about understanding why overwatering (or the conditions that keep soil wet too long) kills this plant faster than underwatering ever could. Check the pot size, keep water off the crown, use fast-draining soil, adjust for light, and water by feel, not by calendar. None of it costs extra money. It's just getting out of the plant's way.
