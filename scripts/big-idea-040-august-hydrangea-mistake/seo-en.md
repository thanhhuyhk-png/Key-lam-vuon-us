# Hydrangea Not Blooming Next Year? Here's the One August Mistake That Kills Every Flower

Hydrangea bloom mistake — After August 1st, next year's flower buds are already forming inside this year's stems. Extension research confirms this critical date. Prune after August 1st on bigleaf or oakleaf hydrangeas, and you're removing invisible flowers you won't even notice missing until June, when your neighbor's hydrangea is covered in blooms.

**YMYL Safety Note:** This video is for educational purposes only and is not medical or veterinary advice. Hydrangeas contain a toxic compound called cyanogenic glycoside. The ASPCA lists hydrangeas as toxic to dogs, cats, and horses. Serious poisoning is rare—most pets that nibble a leaf just get an upset stomach. But if a pet eats a large amount, or you notice vomiting, diarrhea, or unusual lethargy, call your veterinarian or the ASPCA Poison Control at 1-888-426-4435 immediately. If a child ingests any part of a hydrangea, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 or your pediatrician right away.
