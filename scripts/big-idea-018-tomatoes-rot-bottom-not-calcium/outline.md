### PART 1 — THE MYTH (phút ~1–3): "It's not a calcium shortage in your soil"
- Claim chính: BER is *rarely* a lack of calcium in the soil. Most garden soil already has plenty. (UMN, Illinois Ext)
- "Here's why this works" + analogy: calcium is like a passenger that can only ride one bus — **water**. Calcium moves through the plant *only* in the water stream (transpiration). No water moving = calcium stranded, even if the soil is full of it. (BER review / PMC)
- Why the *bottom* (blossom end): leaves sweat (transpire) fast and pull most of the water + calcium toward themselves; the fruit barely sweats, so the far end of the fruit gets shorted first. That's why it's the *blossom end* that rots. (research-backed)
- Callback to channel: "If you saw our eggshell video — same lesson. The calcium was never the missing piece."

### PART 2 — THE REAL TRIGGER (phút ~3–6): inconsistent moisture — 🔴 tension layer phút 4–6
- Root cause = swings in soil moisture: bone-dry then soaked, especially after a dry spell + heavy rain or heavy watering. Heat waves, low humidity, fast growth spurts make it worse. (UMN, PSU)
- Pattern break / open-loop payoff at ~minute 4–5: "the heat-wave mistake" — watering deep one day, skipping three, then drowning them — the swing itself is the damage, not the amount.
- 3 contributing factors most folks miss (keeps the "number three" promise):
  1. Over-fertilizing with high-nitrogen (ammonium) feed → forces fast leafy growth that out-races calcium delivery. (UMass)
  2. Damaged/disturbed roots — hoeing too close, transplant shock → fewer roots = less water uptake. (UMN)
  3. **#3 (the payoff):** letting the bed dry out between waterings in summer heat — the single most common swing, and the one calcium spray can't fix.
- Reassurance (Don't panic): early-season fruit is hit most; the plant itself is healthy and later fruit usually comes in clean once watering steadies.

### PART 3 — THE FIX (phút ~6–11): the watering routine that actually works
- Core fix = *consistency*, not calcium. Steady soil moisture lets the plant move its own calcium.
  - Water deeply and evenly: ~1–2 inches per week, split across the week, not one flood. (multiple Ext)
  - Mulch 2–3 inches (straw, shredded leaves, wood chips) to buffer the swings — the lazy, high-leverage move. (UMN)
  - Drip line / soaker hose / a buried jug for even delivery (callback to milk-jug watering idea); morning watering.
  - Container tomatoes rot more because pots dry fast — check daily in heat. (zone/climate anchor → comment Theme 2)
- The honest truth about calcium products: foliar calcium sprays and soil amendments *rarely* fix BER if the watering is still swinging — leaves barely absorb it and the soil usually wasn't short to begin with. Fix the water first. (UMN home-remedies)
- Money line ($0 framing): "Before you spend a dime on calcium spray — the fix is free. It's a watering can and a handful of mulch."
- Affected fruit can't be saved — pick off the rotted ones so the plant feeds the healthy fruit; the next set comes in clean.

### BONUS TIP (1 câu ít ai nói)
- A quick soil test or even a $15 home test before buying any calcium product — most gardeners find their soil already had enough, confirming the swing (not shortage) was the culprit.

### CONCLUSION (1–2 phút)
- Recap mantra 3-nhịp: "It's not the calcium — it's the *water*, and the *steadiness*."
- 1 actionable step today: mulch the bed and set a simple twice-a-week watering rhythm.
- Pet/family anchor (Theme 3 quick win): straw mulch + even watering is pet- and grandkid-safe — no chemicals in the bed.
- CTA (theo channel-truth — soft, demystify): nhẹ nhàng mời xem video eggshell/calcium để hiểu trọn câu chuyện calcium; đóng bằng `Happy gardening.`
