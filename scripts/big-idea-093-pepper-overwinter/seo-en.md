## TITLE
Don't Pull Your Pepper Plants Yet. They Can Live for Years

## SEO DESCRIPTION

Pepper plants are perennials. Frost ends them, not age, so you can keep one through winter and start next spring with a plant that already has its roots.

This video walks through two ways to overwinter pepper plants indoors: a resting method for a cool, low-light corner (around 50–60°F, cut back to three stems, watered rarely), and a green method for a bright spot with grow lights. Plus the step to do before any pot comes inside, and when it's safe to move the plant back out in spring.

**In this video:**
- Why a pepper plant can live for years in warm zones (9–11) and what that means up north
- How to choose between resting and keeping it green, based on the rooms you have
- The cut: three main stems, leaves off — and two ways extension pages describe it
- The door step: wash the old soil off the roots, then a clean pot and fresh mix
- Aphids, spider mites and whiteflies: what to check, and labeled insecticidal soap (test first)
- Spring: wait until after your last frost, and harden off for about two weeks

What we couldn't find a source for, so we don't guess: how many hours of grow light a green plant needs, and an exact date to wake a resting plant.
