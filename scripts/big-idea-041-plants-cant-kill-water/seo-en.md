Plants that grow in water — no soil, no repotting, no cost. If every houseplant you bring home turns brown, the problem may not be you. It may be the dirt. Here are seven common houseplants that live in a plain glass jar of water, and the honest truth about how long each one really lasts.

In this video you'll learn:
- The seven easiest houseplants to grow in water with no soil at all
- The one rule that works on all seven: nodes under the water, leaves above it
- Why letting tap water sit overnight helps some plants but not lucky bamboo
- The three warning signs of root rot that show up before a single leaf wilts
- Which two of the seven live in water for years, and which five need a pot eventually

Most lists tell you all seven last forever. That isn't true, and this one says so. Two of these plants — pothos and lucky bamboo — are documented growing in water for a year or more with a little liquid plant food. The other five will give you months, sometimes a full season, and then do better in soil. This video also covers something almost nobody mentions: several university extension services actually recommend against rooting plants in water. There's a good reason for that warning, and a good reason it doesn't apply if you never move the plant into a pot.

Every plant here is common in American homes and takes ten minutes to start at the kitchen sink. No kneeling, no hauling bags of soil, and no trip to the garden center — a cutting from a neighbor costs nothing.

Pet owners: six of these seven are on the ASPCA's toxic plant list. That's covered in detail near the end, along with the one that's safe for cats and dogs.
