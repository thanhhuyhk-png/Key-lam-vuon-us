### CATEGORY 1: YARD EATERS — Spread & Invade

**Case File #1: Running Bamboo**
- Why innocent: Exotic privacy screen, "creates privacy fast"
- Year 3: Rhizomes under fence + patio, shoots 20 feet away
- Dollar damage: $450–$5,000+ removal; NJ/PA up to $30,000
- Safe substitute: Clumping bamboo (Fargesia spp.) — same look, no spread

**Case File #2: Mint (any variety)**
- Why innocent: "Just a herb," smells amazing, practical
- Year 3: Stolons colonized 6-foot radius, sprouting through cracks
- Dollar damage: Hours of manual removal annually; contaminates vegetable beds
- Safe substitute: Lemon balm in buried container — same aromatics, controlled

**Case File #3: Bishop's Weed / Goutweed (Aegopodium podagraria)**
- Why innocent: Variegated ornamental groundcover, "fills shady problem areas"
- Year 3: Impenetrable mat, displaces ALL native plants; prohibited in VT and MA
- Dollar damage: $85–$3,316 per site, 3+ years to eradicate
- Safe substitute: Allegheny spurge (Pachysandra procumbens) — native, zones 5–9

---

### CATEGORY 2: TIME THIEVES — Beautiful but Relentless

**Case File #4: Wisteria (Asian varieties)**
- Why innocent: Stunning purple cascading flowers, fragrant, "elegant cottage vine"
- Year 3: Girdled small tree, requires weekly pruning to keep off roof
- Dollar damage: 5 years to control with herbicide; structural damage to pergolas
- Safe substitute: American wisteria (Wisteria frutescens) — same flowers, native, less aggressive

**Case File #5: English Ivy**
- Why innocent: Classic evergreen groundcover, "requires no maintenance"
- Year 3: Climbed mature trees, extra weight causes branch failure; kills understory
- Dollar damage: $85–$3,316+ per site (UMD Extension); "highly aggressive invader"
- Safe substitute: Virginia creeper (Parthenocissus quinquefolia) — native, fall color, non-strangling

**Case File #6: Purple Loosestrife**
- Why innocent: Tall purple flowers, "attracts pollinators," wildflower look
- Year 3: 2.7 million seeds per plant annually; transforms wetlands into monocultures; illegal in many states
- Dollar damage: Near-impossible to eradicate; destroys wildlife habitat
- Safe substitute: Native blazing star (Liatris spicata) — same purple drama, real pollinator value

---

### CATEGORY 3: HIDDEN HAZARDS — Nobody Warns You

**Case File #7: Lily of the Valley**
- Why innocent: Delicate white bells, sweet fragrance, classic grandmother's garden plant
- The problem: Entire plant toxic — cardiac symptoms if ingested; dangerous for children and pets
- Dollar damage: Emergency vet or ER visit; spreads aggressively via rhizomes
- Safe substitute: Solomon's seal (Polygonatum) — same woodland elegance, non-toxic

**Case File #8: Rue (Ruta graveolens)** ← EMOTIONAL ANCHOR
- Why innocent: Blue-green ornamental foliage, sold as companion plant and herb; smells interesting
- The science: Contains furanocoumarins (bergapten, psoralen, xanthotoxin). Skin contact + UV-A sunlight (320–380nm) = phototoxic blistering burns. Not on any nursery tag.
- The story: Child brushes arm against plant → goes outside → develops burns within hours. Looks like cooking burn. Days to understand source.
- Dollar damage: Urgent care visit; potential scarring without treatment
- Safe substitute: Catmint (Nepeta) — same blue-grey foliage, pest-deterrent, safe for children and pets
