## Etiolated Stem "Can It Thicken?" — Honest Assessment

**What T1-T3 sources confirm:**
- Once a leaf turns yellow it "won't green up again" (C15, UMD T1) — permanent cellular damage applies to color loss too.
- ISU (T1) says cut back leggy stems as a remedy; it does not say stems will recover on their own.

**What only Tier 4 source says (Laidback Gardener blog, no research citation):**
- For a bare-stemmed aloe with 12-inch (30 cm) of bare stem: cut the top off 2 inches (5 cm) below the lowest leaves, expose adventitious root bumps, reroot in barely moist mix, place in well-lit spot; "In 4 or 5 months, your aloe will be a picture of health again."
- Source URL: https://laidbackgardener.blog/2018/11/03/how-to-save-a-bare-stemmed-aloe/
- VERDICT: Use this technique in the video but hedge it as "what experienced growers recommend" (Tier 4 language); do NOT claim a T1 source supports beheading specifically.

**Botanical note (no T1 source for aloe specifically; general monocot physiology from non-T1 source):**
- Aloe vera is a monocot (family Asphodelaceae, order Asparagales). Most monocots lack a vascular cambium, so mature stems cannot add secondary vascular tissue → existing etiolated stems physically CANNOT thicken by cell division. New growth from the crown will be compact under better light. This is the physiological truth but requires hedging in video: [NEED DATA: T1 source explicitly confirming aloe stem anatomy and absence of secondary thickening].

## Source Disagree on Sunburn vs Low-Light Leaf Color

**Sunburn/sun stress (orange/red/brown leaves):** Multiple non-T1 sources describe this as anthocyanin-based protective pigmentation reversible if plant is moved; sunburn (bleached/leathery patches) is permanent. **No T1 source found** describing this visual distinction for aloe specifically.

**Low-light pale (gray-green, washed-out):** ISU T1: "lanky and pale" (C04); UMD T1: "too much light is a rare problem" indoors (C17). Direction confirmed; exact color description [NEED DATA: T1 source].
